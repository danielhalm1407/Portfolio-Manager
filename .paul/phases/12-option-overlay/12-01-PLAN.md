<objective>
## Goal

Three hedge structures from the Obsidian catalogue — protective put, put spread, rolling collar —
expressed as executable rules that slot into the existing `RebalanceRule.propose` contract, so
the Phase 2 simulator accounts them unchanged.

## Goal, stated as a number

Given a synthetic vol surface derived from the Phase 11 history, the rolling collar on SPX must
price correctly for at least three historical 63-day windows covering different regimes. If it
does not, this plan has not succeeded, regardless of how much scaffolding was written.

## Purpose

Phase 12 is the second phase of milestone v0.4. Phase 11 established that the hedge structures
can be priced — it recorded the provenance of the vol data and answered whether an implied surface
is obtainable or whether realised/VIX vol must be synthesised. Phase 12 takes that answer and
builds the pricing engine and roll mechanics. Without this, Phase 13 cannot run a walk-forward
sweep that includes any option leg; the v0.4 claim is not testable with a collar in it.

The mechanism under test in v0.4 is: a hedge leg that rallies into an equity drawdown is SOLD
into that rally by the rebalance, generating cash redeployed into the fallen growth leg near the
trough. A collar caps the upside of that rally but also limits the downside cost. Phase 13 will
ask whether the net improvement in risk-adjusted returns justifies the theta drag; Phase 12
makes that question answerable.

## Output

`OptionLeg` as a synthetic-instrument descriptor, a reusable `RollCalendar`, and three
concrete rules — `ProtectivePutRule`, `PutSpreadRule`, `RollingCollarRule` — composing the
Phase 16 stages so `PortfolioSimulator` accounts them unchanged. Tests run offline against
fixtures, split by stage. `COVERAGE.md` updated with a vol-provenance note. See the
supersession note below for which module each piece lands in.

## AC-1: OptionLeg is a first-class pricing primitive
```gherkin
Given a strike, expiry, right (put/call), underlying spot and a vol input
When OptionLeg.price() is called
Then it returns a mark-to-model premium from EUROPEAN Black-Scholes
And the premium is non-negative and never below intrinsic value,
     where intrinsic for a put is the DISCOUNTED floor max(K*exp(-rT) - S, 0)
     and for a call is max(S - K*exp(-rT), 0)
And no clamp is applied to enforce that floor, because Black-Scholes already satisfies it
```

**Terminology, fixed here so the rest of the plan is unambiguous (2026-08-26).**
*Intrinsic value* in this plan means the **discounted** quantity `K*exp(-rT) - S` (put).
*Exercise value* means the undiscounted `K - S` — what exercising right now would pay.
For a European put the two differ, and the ordering is:

    intrinsic (K*e^-rT - S)  <=  option price  <=  ... and exercise value (K - S) may sit ABOVE the price

A deep-ITM European put legitimately prices BELOW its exercise value, because the cash it
owes you cannot be collected until expiry. Worked example — S=100, K=150, T=2y, r=5%,
vol=20%: Black-Scholes = 38.06, exercise value = 50.00, intrinsic floor = 35.73. The price
sits 11.94 below exercise value and 2.34 above the intrinsic floor, and that 2.34 is
exactly the matching call price — put-call parity gives
`put - (K*e^-rT - S) = call >= 0`, which is *why* Black-Scholes cannot breach the floor.

**Therefore: do not write `max(bs, K - S)`.** Clamping to exercise value would price a
deep-ITM put ABOVE fair European value — in this example returning 50.00 for an option
worth 38.06 — handing the book ~12 points of phantom hedge value in precisely the
deep-drawdown states Phase 13 exists to measure. The floor is an invariant to ASSERT,
never an operation to apply.

## AC-2: Roll calendar fires on schedule
```gherkin
Given a RollingCollarRule initialised with a 63-TRADING-day reset and a history panel
When the simulator advances 63 bars through the panel
Then the rule rolls: closes the expiring legs, strikes the replacement legs to the period-start spot,
     and records the debit or credit in the order ledger as a separate Fill per leg
And the next roll fires 63 bars later regardless of intervening rebalances
```

**Trading days, not calendar days (2026-08-26).** The reset is counted in BARS of the
price panel, not in `timedelta` days. Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It matches the source.** `bse.py:4134` `simulate_hedge_strategy_from_underlying`
   walks `start + reset_days` as integer indices into a path array with
   `trading_days_per_year=252` — `HEDGE_RESET_DAYS = 63` there is a quarter of trading
   days, i.e. ~91 calendar days, not 63.
2. **It is simpler here.** The rule already receives one bar at a time from
   `PortfolioSimulator.run()`; a bar counter needs no date arithmetic, no weekend or
   holiday handling, and no market-calendar dependency — which is what the original
   "no market-calendar dependency" instinct was actually reaching for.
3. **It cannot silently desynchronise from the panel.** A calendar-day roll can land on
   a date the panel has no row for, and the roll would then either be skipped or fire on
   an arbitrary neighbouring bar. A bar count cannot drift from the data it indexes.

`t` for pricing is still a genuine year fraction: `remaining_bars / 252.0`, matching
`crash.py:1901` (`time_to_expiry = remaining_days / 252.0`).

## AC-3: All three structures plug into the existing propose() contract
```gherkin
Given PortfolioSimulator configured with a ProtectivePutRule, PutSpreadRule or RollingCollarRule
When simulator.run() is called
Then propose() returns {symbol: units} for option legs alongside equity legs
And the Fill accounting for option premium flows through the existing fills.py primitives unchanged
And the realised P&L from a closed option leg is attributed to the hedge sleeve
And each option-leg Fill carries the rationale that opened it, via the 02-02 channel
```

**`propose()` returns UNITS, not weights (2026-08-26).** The contract at
`rules.py:79-83` is *"Return {symbol: signed delta units to trade at this bar}"*. Weights
are rule-INTERNAL: they come from config and are converted by `weights_to_units()`
(`rules.py:44`) before `propose` returns. The full chain is

    config weights -> weights_to_units() -> {symbol: delta units} -> simulator merges
      -> backend.execute() (execution.py:45) -> Fill (execution.py:58) -> ledger

Option legs SKIP the first hop entirely: contracts are natively units, and there is no
weight representation of "long 3 SPX 4750 puts" to convert from. This is why the
`propose()` contract fits option legs better than it first appears — but see the
rationale dependency below, which is the part that does not fit yet.

**Rationale must ride into the Fill, and that needs 02-02 first.** AC-2 wants four Fills
per collar roll, each tagged with its structure and reason. Today that is impossible:
`Fill` (fills.py:25-45) has no rationale field, and `Order` (fills.py:63-79) has one
keyed to the `dummy_orders.json` schema but is **never constructed** in `src/portutils/`.
`PortfolioSimulator` calls `execute` with no `ctx` and `on_order=None` — the gap ROADMAP
L104-105 records as the reason Phase 2 was reopened. Phase 12 therefore consumes 02-02's
expanded Fill/Order structure rather than building a parallel one. Where a structure has
an intermediate weighting step (notional sizing, spread scaling), that weighting belongs
in the same rationale payload, so the Fill explains not just WHY the leg was opened but
how it was sized.

    **Whichever option is selected, the vol input MUST be a function of strike, not a scalar.**

    A single vol per bar makes `PutSpreadRule` economically meaningless: the structure only
    cheapens protection because the sold lower strike carries HIGHER implied vol than the
    bought upper strike. Price both legs off one flat number and the spread's entire
    rationale — selling the expensive tail into the skew — disappears, leaving a strictly
    worse protective put. The same flat-vol error would make the collar's short call look
    cheaper than it is.

    So `OptionLeg.price()` takes a vol that has already been evaluated AT THAT LEG'S STRIKE.
    The surface itself is a pipeline concern, passed in; the leg never fetches it.

    **Starting point: `crash.py:2579` `synthetic_iv_surface`.** Port it as-is, then
    recalibrate the constants against whatever Phase 11 actually produced. It IS the
    "fixed skew slope" that Option B describes, already written:

        def synthetic_iv_surface(strike, tau, spot_ref):
            # Equity-like: lower strikes carry higher IV (put skew / smirk),
            # longer expiries slightly more IV, mild convexity keeps it surface-like.
            log_moneyness = np.log(strike / spot_ref)
            base  = 0.16                                     # ATM level  <- replace with the Phase 11 series
            term  = 0.06 * np.sqrt(np.maximum(tau, 1e-6))    # term structure
            skew  = -0.24 * log_moneyness                    # THE skew term: K < S  =>  higher IV
            smile =  0.30 * log_moneyness ** 2               # convexity
            return np.clip(base + term + skew + smile, 0.10, 0.85)

    dynamic one.**

    The surface has two terms that can be made dynamic, and they are independent:

    | Term | Static form (crash.py) | Dynamic form |
    |---|---|---|

    Port the function with `spot_ref` pinned to the PERIOD-START spot and `base` left at the

    Three reasons it is the right starting point:

    1. **It carries no Phase 11 dependency.** `base = 0.16` needs no vol history at all, so

       data phase gates the RESULT, not the BUILD — see the sequencing note below.
    2. **It is the source's own calibration.** Every `synthetic_iv_surface` call site in
       `crash.py` passes `spot_ref=SPOT0`, and the constants were chosen against that
       convention. Changing the reference without recalibrating the constants is not
       "porting it as-is", it is a different model wearing the same numbers.
    3. **It is honest about what it is.** A frozen surface makes every move in a leg's value
       attributable to spot and time decay alone, which is easy to reason about and easy to
       unit-test. `crash.py`'s own caveat block names this limitation explicitly.

    Its known bias, stated so it is never a surprise: implied vol never rises as the market

    - Subclasses `RebalanceRule` so `PortfolioSimulator` needs no changes

    - Expiry: one roll-calendar period forward
    - Does NOT write a call — pure downside protection, maximum cost

    - Buys the put as above AND writes a put `spread_width` lower (e.g., 0.80 = second strike at

      silently swap
    - Net premium is long-put cost minus short-put credit; record both legs individually in the
      Fill ledger so the gross and net are separately visible

    **RollingCollarRule**
    - Buys the put as above AND writes a call `upside_cap` above spot. **Default 1.28 —
      cap upside at +28% per period** — matching `HEDGE_UPSIDE_CAP = 0.28` at `bse.py:3941`.

      for the floor at −10%, and it is the ratio the source actually ran. A tight cap
      (the previously specified 1.05) is a different structure — closer to a short-vol
      position with a floor attached — and must not be the default
    - 63-day reset per `RollCalendar`; strikes re-struck to period-start spot, as "How to roll a
      put hedge" specifies
    - At roll: close put (buy back) + close call (buy back), then open new legs — four Fills per
      roll event, each with rationale "collar roll"

    **Redeployment — `RollingCollarRule` only (scoped 2026-08-26)**

    The settlement-and-redeploy half of `bse.py:4196-4218`. This is the mechanic ROADMAP
    L243 names as the thing v0.4 exists to test — *"a hedge leg that rallies into an equity
    drawdown is SOLD into that rally by the rebalance, generating cash that is redeployed
    into the fallen growth leg near the trough"* — so the collar cannot ship without it.

    At each roll bar, AFTER closing the expiring legs and BEFORE opening the new ones:

    1. **Settle.** `net_cash_flow` = closing value of the long put − closing value of the
       short call − premium paid to open the replacement pair. Positive = the floor paid
       out; negative = the cap was breached and the structure owes money.
    2. **Redeploy on a bad period.** If `net_cash_flow > 0` AND the period return
       (`end_spot / period_start_spot - 1`) is at or below `redeploy_trigger`
       (default **−0.05**, per `HEDGE_REDEPLOY_TRIGGER`), convert `redeploy_rate` of that
       cash (default **1.00**) into additional units of the underlying growth leg at the
       CURRENT bar price. These units are returned in the same `propose()` delta dict as
       the option legs, so the simulator executes them as ordinary equity buys and the
       Fill lands in the blotter with rationale "collar redeploy".
       This is the whole point: buying MORE shares at the depressed price is where the
       edge comes from, not the hedge payoff itself.
    3. **Fund a deficit.** If `net_cash_flow < 0`, sell underlying units to cover, capped
       at 99.9% of the current position (`bse.py:4210`) so the book can never be driven to
       zero or negative units by a breached cap. Rationale "collar funding".
    4. **No redeploy on a good period.** If the period return is above the trigger, the
       cash simply accrues — it is NOT spent. The rule holds it and re-tests at the next
       roll.

    Both the trigger and the rate belong in `option_overlay` config, not hardcoded: Phase 13
    sweeps them. Deviation from source to note in a comment: `bse.py` marks legs at pure
