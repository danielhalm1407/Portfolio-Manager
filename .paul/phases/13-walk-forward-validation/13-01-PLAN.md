### 1. THE MONETISE / REOPEN THRASH — the biggest of the four
Rung 3c monetises and reopens on CONSECUTIVE BARS, repeatedly. Measured over the GFC window with
this plan's own illustrative levels (`monetise_drawdown=0.10`, `reentry_iv=0.18`,
`max_flat_bars=126`):

```
2008-05-07  monetise  drawdown 0.1081  iv 0.1826
2008-05-08  reopen    SPY P 125.24     iv 0.1778
2008-05-09  monetise  drawdown 0.1121  iv 0.1810
2008-05-12  reopen    SPY P 126.41     iv 0.1699
2008-05-13  monetise  drawdown 0.1020  iv 0.1683
```

Over the full 5,201-bar history that is **281 monetisations against 48 rolls** — the policy spends
its life thrashing rather than hedging, and every "monetisation" statistic this plan's AC-5 table
reports would be counting that.

**Mechanism.** `_gate_open()` tests implied vol against `reentry_iv` and NOTHING ELSE. It does not
test whether the monetise trigger is still firing. Mid-2008 IV sat at 0.16-0.18, so the gate opened
on the very next bar, the rule re-struck into a market still more than 10% below its peak, and
`_monetise_trigger` fired again immediately. `_peak` does not reset on a monetise, so the drawdown
condition stays true for as long as the market is below its old peak — which after a real crash is
years.

**This is not obviously a defect in the state machine.** Both gates demonstrably work: the
2009-03-03 reopen fires at exactly 126 flat bars, so `max_flat_bars` is correct, and the IV gate
does hold where IV is genuinely elevated. Two candidate readings, and Task 3 must decide between
them rather than patch:

  a. **`reentry_iv = 0.18` is simply the wrong illustrative level.** It was hand-picked, and 0.18
     is close to SPY's unconditional median IV — so it is not a "vol is calm again" test at all,
     it is very nearly an always-open gate. A level materially below the crisis regime would not
     thrash. If this is the whole story, the fix is a parameter, not code, and it belongs in
     13-02's sweep rather than here.
  b. **The gate is missing a DRAWDOWN-RECOVERED condition.** A re-entry test on vol alone asks
     "is insurance cheap again", when the policy also needs "is the loss event over". A second
     condition — drawdown back above some fraction of the trigger, or a fresh peak — would stop a
     reopen landing inside the same drawdown that just triggered. This is a code change to
     `_gate_open()` and a new parameter, so it is an amendment to this plan's Task 2, not a side
     effect of Task 3.

Reading (b) is the more likely one on the evidence, because (a) alone does not explain a reopen
landing one bar after a monetise — any IV level low enough to be a real gate would still be
crossed eventually, and the thrash would recur there. But (a) is cheap to test first and should
be: run rung 3c at two or three `reentry_iv` levels before writing any new gate code.

#### RESOLVED 2026-09-21 — neither (a) nor (b): the drawdown REFERENCE resets on reopen
Decided with the user and applied in `07615c7`. `_gate_open()` is UNCHANGED; `reentry_iv` keeps its
level. `_maybe_reopen` now sets `self._peak = spot` alongside the `_period_start_bar` and
`_long_premium` resets already there
(`../../src/portutils/strategies/rules/options.py`).

**Why this and not (b).** The drawdown reference was the ONLY piece of the rule's state that
survived a monetise it had nothing to do with. Everything else already restarts at the reopen: the
legs are struck at `floor x spot` off that bar, `_long_premium` resets, `_period_start_bar` resets.
`_multiple_now` divides by `_long_premium` and is therefore ALREADY entry-relative, so the two
triggers AC-3b is meant to compare were answering different questions — one "has this hedge paid",
the other "is the market below an all-time high". Resetting `_peak` makes them commensurable, which
AC-3b needs before its comparison means anything. Adding a drawdown-recovered condition to the gate
would have left that inconsistency in place and papered over it with a second parameter.

**Reading (a) is not dismissed, it is DEMOTED.** With the reference reset, the thrash is gone
structurally and `reentry_iv` goes back to being a "don't buy dear insurance" filter. Its level is
now a legitimate 13-02 sweep parameter rather than a correctness question, which is where the plan
wanted it.

**ON REOPEN, NOT ON MONETISE — measured, not assumed.** Both were run on a 85-bar 100 -> 55 decline
with `max_flat_bars=10` and the IV gate pinned shut, so every reopen is forced and the market keeps
falling through the flat period:

| reset point | monetise bars | bars from reopen to the next monetise |
|---|---|---|
| **reopen (adopted)** | 23, 48, 71 | 15, 13 |
| monetise | 23, 39, 54, 67, 79 | 6, 5, 3, 2 |

Resetting at the monetise counts the fall that happens WHILE FLAT — during which there is no hedge
to monetise — against the next hedge's trigger. The gaps shrink as the decline steepens and the
thrash creeps back. Resetting at the reopen asks the one question the policy means to ask: has the
market fallen `monetise_drawdown` since the hedge currently held was struck.

**NOT reset on a roll.** A roll re-strikes at `floor x current spot`, so the same argument could be
made for it — and it is deliberately declined. A roll continues the same hedge programme rather than
banking a gain and deciding to re-insure, and resetting every `reset_bars` would stop the trigger
ever firing on a slow grind down, because the reference would keep stepping down with the market.
The asymmetry is intentional and is commented at the reset site so it does not read as an oversight.

**AC-1 is unaffected, with no flag.** `_maybe_reopen` is reachable only from `FLAT`, `FLAT` is set
only in the monetise branch, and `_state` initialises to `HEDGED` — so the line is unreachable
unless a monetise trigger is armed, and every default run is byte-identical.

**Guarded by test**, `test_a_reopen_resets_the_drawdown_reference_so_the_trigger_cannot_refire_next_bar`
in `../../tests/test_option_monetisation.py`, verified to FAIL without the change. One existing test
had been encoding the defect: `test_drawdown_trigger_closes_on_the_bar_the_threshold_is_crossed`
asserted `_state == "FLAT"` at the END of its path, which held only because the rule was thrashing on
alternate bars and the last bar happened to be a monetise. Its stated intent is unchanged and still
passes; the trailing assertion now checks the monetise marker on the trigger bar.

### 2. THE RESIDUAL TIMING TERM IS IN `Book`, NOT THE LEDGER
13-01.1 removed the per-leg LEDGER COLUMNS and one run went from ~6.5 min to ~6.3 s. What is left
is a smaller, separate defect, and it is worth naming precisely because the two are easy to
conflate: **`Book` and `StateLedger` are different objects.** `Book` is live state (a dict of
`Position`s, signed qty, VWAP entry, realised P&L); `StateLedger` is the per-bar recording written
FROM it. 13-01.1 narrowed the recording. It did not touch the book, and the book is where the rest
of the cost is.

Measured: ms/bar under `NarrowLedger` is 0.33 / 0.47 / 0.47 / 0.36 across 250 / 500 / 1,000 /
2,000 bars — flat — but **1.21 ms/bar over the full 5,201**, roughly 3x the 2,000-bar figure. The
cause is that `Book.position()` (`book.py:195-202`) auto-creates a `Position` and nothing ever
removes one, so `Book.unrealised()` (`:237-239`) and `Book.gross_exposure()` (`:245-259`) each walk
~82 dead legs on every one of 5,201 bars, and `NarrowLedger.record_book` walks them once more.

That is **candidate fix 2 in `13-01-IMPLEMENTATION-CONTEXT.md` section 6** — retire a `Position`
once it is flat and its leg has expired. 13-01.1 explicitly declined it: it changes
`book.symbols` semantics and therefore everything that iterates the book, and it was not needed to
make a run affordable. It is now the only remaining term, so it is worth doing here IF the measured
saving justifies the blast radius — measure first, at 5,201 bars, before writing anything. 6.3 s is
already affordable, so this is an optimisation with a real regression risk attached, not a blocker.

#### RESOLVED 2026-09-21 — skip flat positions; do NOT retire them
Applied in `ff2caed`. `Book.unrealised`, `Book.gross_exposure`
(`../../src/portutils/portfolio/book.py`) and the
`NarrowLedger.record_book` loop (`../../src/portutils/portfolio/narrow_ledger.py`)
skip positions with zero qty instead of walking every leg ever traded.

**Retirement was rejected for a reason this plan did not name.** `Book.realised` sums `p.realised`
across `_positions`, so DELETING a `Position` deletes the P&L it booked. Any retirement scheme needs
an accumulator to carry that out first, plus handling for `Position.restore`, which writes `realised`
directly and bypasses `apply_fill`. The skip needs none of it: `Position.unrealised` already returns
0.0 at qty 0 and `abs(qty * px)` is 0, so the sums are identical by construction, `book.symbols` keeps
its meaning and `realised` is untouched. It also removes a latent NaN path in `gross_exposure`, where
a dead leg with a NaN `avg_entry` could reach the total through the entry-price fallback. For the
record, this plan's stated blast radius was also overestimated: only three call sites iterate
`book.symbols`, and `ibkr_sync.py:98` already wants it pruned.

**The ledger loop needed a WIDER guard than the other two.** `closed_this_bar` is not gated on qty,
and a leg closed on THIS bar is already flat when `record_book` runs, so a skip on qty alone would
silently drop the closed units `TOTAL_closed_units` — and the turnover series built from it — depends
on. The guard tests qty AND `closed_this_bar`, with a test pinning it.

**Measured, 5,201 bars, protective put rolling at 63 with `monetise_drawdown=0.10`, best of 3 on a
SYNTHETIC path** (so the ratios are the finding, the absolutes are not):

| | time | symbols in book | monetisations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| neither fix | 14.87 s | 2,309 | 2,305 |
| this skip only | 3.40 s | 2,309 | 2,305 |
| the reopen reset only | 1.81 s | 106 | 52 |
| both | 1.38 s | 106 | 52 |

**Finding 2 was largely a symptom of finding 1.** The reopen reset is worth 8.2x here, not by making
anything faster but by collapsing the dead-leg COUNT; this skip adds 1.31x on top of it. An estimate
of ~3.3x for the skip alone, extrapolated from the ms/bar figures above rather than measured, was too
high — the whole-run saving is 23-27% once the book is not thrashing.

**The O(1) `realised` accumulator is DECLINED.** `Book.realised` still walks every position and is the
one walk this skip cannot remove, but it is a cheap walk — plain attribute access, where the two
skipped walks each pay `_resolve_price` and a finiteness branch per symbol. In isolation the skip takes
the per-bar book cost from 0.1447 to 0.0185 ms/bar; making `realised` O(1) only reaches 0.0132. The last
15% of an already small residual is not worth an invariant that `Position.restore` would silently break.

## The policy (decided with the user 2026-09-20)
A three-state cycle per option rule:

```
HEDGED --(monetise trigger fires)----------> monetise: close every leg --> FLAT
FLAT   --(IV has fallen to the re-entry gate)---------> reopen fresh legs off TODAY's spot --> HEDGED
HEDGED --(trigger never fires by expiry)-----------> roll as today (close, reopen) --> HEDGED
```

- **Monetise, on either of two triggers.** The **drawdown** trigger is PRIMARY (amended 2026-09-20):
  close when the underlying is `monetise_drawdown` below its running peak, because that is the loss
  event the insurance exists for. The **value multiple** — long-leg value divided by premium paid,
  at or above `monetise_multiple` — is retained as the COMPARATOR. Both are built; the batch run
  reports how often the two fire on the same bar, which is the hypothesis under test.
- **Wait for cheap insurance.** After monetising, stay unhedged until implied vol has fallen to a
  re-entry level. "No point buying over-priced insurance": a vol spike that made the old put pay is
  the same spike that would make the new one dear.
- **Reopen, then the same rule again.** The moment the position reopens, it is back in the HEDGED
  state: monetise early if the trigger fires, otherwise roll at expiry.

**Per-bar persistence, added 2026-09-20.** Every run must write its **per-bar equity / P&L series**,
keyed by the configuration, not just summary statistics. This is what lets 13-02 and 13-03 score any
window as a slice rather than re-running the simulator per origin, collapsing their cost from
(grid x origins) runs to (grid) runs. Max drawdown is NOT sliceable from an aggregate, so it must be
derivable from the stored series. Building this here, in the engine, is the single decision that
makes the later plans affordable.

## Decisions

**2026-09-21 — the drawdown reference resets on REOPEN, not on monetise, and never on a roll.**
The plan offered two readings of the thrash (a wrong `reentry_iv` level, or a drawdown-recovered
condition in `_gate_open()`); the decision went a third way and left the gate untouched. Reset at the
reopen rather than at the monetise because resetting at the monetise counts the fall that happens
while FLAT — when there is no hedge to monetise — against the next hedge's trigger, and the measured
gaps shrink as the decline steepens (6, 5, 3, 2 bars against 15, 13). Not reset on a roll because a
roll continues the same hedge programme rather than banking a gain, and a reference that stepped down
every `reset_bars` would stop the trigger ever firing on a slow grind down. Full evidence in the
`RESOLVED` block under finding 1.

**2026-09-21 — `reentry_iv` is demoted from a correctness question to a 13-02 sweep parameter.**
It was load-bearing for thrash prevention only because the drawdown reference was stale. With the
reference reset the thrash is gone structurally, so the gate does the one job it was written for.

**2026-09-21 — flat positions are SKIPPED in `Book`, not retired.**
Retirement (candidate fix 2 in the implementation context) would delete each retired `Position`'s
realised P&L, since `Book.realised` sums across `_positions`. The skip is identical by construction
and touches no semantics. The O(1) `realised` accumulator was declined as the last 15% of an already
small residual, for an invariant `Position.restore` would silently break.

**2026-09-21 — finding 2 is recorded as largely a SYMPTOM of finding 1.**
The reopen reset is worth 8.2x on a monetising config by collapsing the dead-leg count; the skip adds
1.31x on top. 13-02's grid should be sized from a run measured AFTER both, on real data, not from the
6.3 s figure or from the synthetic numbers in this plan.
